Before beginning my master’s degree, I earned my bachelor’s degree in the Qiming Experimental Class (Seed Class) at HUST’s School of Electronic Information and Communications. During my undergraduate studies, I was also a member of Dian Group, where I worked with Yayu Gao. I also spent a summer as a visiting student working with Boyd Anderson’s robotics group at the School of Computing, National University of Singapore.

My research goal is to help robotic arms move more efficiently and reliably in uncertain real-world environments, for example, making better use of their redundant degrees of freedom. I value systems that are both simple and effective. In particular, I am interested in foundational algorithms and abstractions. I sometimes arrive at perspectives that are uniquely my own, and these insights have helped me discover the distinctive joy of research.

In the long term, I hope to design and build small robots that address practical problems in daily life.

FWIK: Multi-Waypoint IK Selection via Lookahead Graph Search

Jinglan Cao, Guohui Zhong, Chengwei Zhang

arXiv preprint, 2026

FWIK selects inverse-kinematics solutions by considering a short window of future waypoints and screening likely self-collisions before downstream motion planning.

arXiv / PDF

Insights behind FWIK
  1. Short-horizon lookahead. Many manipulation tasks expose a short sequence of future goals, making limited lookahead both possible and useful.
  2. Redundancy as a resource. A manipulator’s kinematic redundancy can be spent preparing its configuration for the next action.
  3. Bounded horizons. The value of future information decays with distance, so a small window can capture much of the benefit of full-sequence optimization at lower cost.
  4. Reachability is not continuity. A nearby IK solution may still require detours, reversals, or collision-prone transitions, while a slightly farther solution can yield smoother and faster motion.
